## Abstract

PRL-8-53 is an obscure substituted aminoethyl benzoate for which historical behavioral reports are often discussed without a clearly established molecular mechanism. We conducted a bounded audit of retained chemical, literature, patent, database, structural, and computational evidence, followed by limited docking checks in preselected protein models. The historical material actually inspected comprised a 1975 patent and the abstract—but not full text—of a 1978 double-blind human study; only citation metadata, not the article body, was available for a 1974 preclinical report. The inspected patent and 1978 abstract report behavioral or tissue-level observations and do not measure binding to a molecular target. No claim is made about the uninspected 1974 article body. Bounded database and literature searches did not identify a direct PRL-8-53 affinity measurement or a compound-specific pKa, but this is not exhaustive evidence of absence.

SwissTargetPrediction placed acetyl-CoA carboxylase 2 (ACC2/ACACB) first and produced additional hypotheses including the μ-opioid receptor, two potassium channels, butyrylcholinesterase, and the serotonin transporter (SERT). The service output is conditional model evidence rather than a PRL-8-53 binding probability. The neutral and protonated queries returned byte-identical 100-target rankings and are therefore not independent replications; this does not establish general insensitivity to protonation. Source ligands, assays, and publications overlap across predictions. An ACC2 native-ligand redocking pilot reproducibly top-ranked the wrong orientation, about 9 Å from the deposited geometry, while a closer deposited-like family was not recovered consistently. The planned PRL-8-53 stage was therefore stopped, and no PRL-8-53/ACC2 score exists. A separate paroxetine-only SERT pilot recovered the deposited paroxetine geometry as the closest, top-ranked mode in six searches, supporting only the internal geometry of that exact model. Twelve subsequent fixed PRL-8-53 searches generated 240 modes and 14,280 within-state pose comparisons. One neutral preparation was strongly seed-sensitive, whereas the sampled protonated preparations varied less; neither observation establishes a physiological protonation preference or SERT binding.

The evidence supports an auditable shortlist of mechanistic hypotheses, not target discovery or confirmation. No PRL-8-53 binding target, calibrated biological target ordering, affinity, efficacy, selectivity, or safety conclusion is established.

## Introduction

PRL-8-53 is methyl 3-[2-[benzyl(methyl)amino]ethyl]benzoate, historically supplied as a hydrochloride salt. The free base is recorded as PubChem CID 39989 (CAS 51352-88-6), while disconnected and ionic hydrochloride records are CIDs 70700868 and 39988 (CAS 51352-87-5). The distinction matters computationally: a disconnected salt record is not a covalent nitrogen–chlorine species, and a neutral free base and a protonated molecular ion are different modeling assumptions. The bounded evidence reviewed here contained no measured compound-specific pKa, so neither neutral nor protonated fractions were treated as experimentally established.

The historical content actually inspected concerns effects above the molecular-target level. The abstract of Hansl and Mead's 1978 human study describes a double-blind serial-anticipation experiment, slight improvement in acquisition, statistically significant improvement in retention, and no significant change in visual reaction time or motor control relative to placebo [1]. The full paper was not accessed, so participant count, dose, timing, task details, and follow-up intervals repeated in secondary accounts were not independently verified. Citation metadata, but no abstract or full text, was available for a 1974 preclinical report [2]; its uninspected contents are not characterized here. A complete inventor patent reports rabbit-ileum spasmolytic observations, rat avoidance and maze observations, and an approximate oral mouse LD50 of 500–700 mg/kg [3]. The inspected abstract and patent observations do not identify a binding target, and the patent toxicity statement is not a clinical safety assessment.

This study asked a narrower question: what molecular targets remain plausible hypotheses after tracing the available evidence and subjecting selected structural models to bounded computational checks? The objective was not to infer a clinical mechanism from behavior, to convert similarity into affinity, or to claim that a docking score is a biological measurement. We separately tracked evidence type and access level because metadata, an abstract, a patent disclosure, a curated activity row, coordinates, and a generated model output answer different questions.

## Methods

### Bounded evidence review and identity control

Chemical identity was reconciled across retained PubChem records and US Patent 3,870,715 [3]. Searches recorded in the repository covered ChEMBL 37, PubChem BioAssay, PubMed, Europe PMC, and bounded checks of BindingDB, DrugBank Online, and the IUPHAR/BPS Guide to PHARMACOLOGY. The ChEMBL searches returned no molecule for the free-base or salt InChIKey, preferred name, or exact synonym and no compounds at the specified ≥70% Tanimoto similarity query. PubChem BioAssay returned “No assay data found” for all three CIDs. PubMed returned one record for the exact term PRL-8-53. Endpoint behavior and unsuccessful or unauthorized queries were recorded rather than interpreted as universal absence. Full historical papers not accessed in the study were not represented as reviewed full text.

### Target prediction and source-ligand audit

Predictions for neutral and explicitly protonated PRL-8-53 against human targets were obtained from the SwissTargetPrediction web server [12–14]. The result field labeled `Probability*` was interpreted according to the service's conditional assumption that the query is bioactive, not as an absolute probability of physical binding. Source ligands were traced to retained ChEMBL 37 [15] molecule, activity, assay, target, and document records and, where available, patent or abstract-level primary sources. Shared compounds, chemical series, and publications were treated as dependence rather than corroboration.

The prediction-service output itself is not redistributed with this work; the target identities carried forward, and the reasoning for each, are given in the target shortlist. All downstream evidence is ChEMBL, UniProt, RCSB PDB, and patent material retained in full.

### Structural triage

Official retained RCSB, wwPDB, UniProt, and Chemical Component Dictionary records were audited for ACC2, human SERT, and human μ-opioid receptor structures. Metadata were distinguished from deposited coordinates and from map-level evidence. Coordinate checks inventoried assemblies, modeled sequence, ligand copies, occupancy, nearby residues and components, and missing atoms. Experimental map files—X-ray electron-density maps or cryo-EM reconstructions, as applicable—were not downloaded or inspected. Four files formerly named as primary-literature excerpts and an old OPRM1 alignment were found to be generated or contradictory records; they were withdrawn as evidence and removed, and their unsupported claims were not used.

Human SERT structure 6VRH, with paroxetine, was selected conditionally for one reference pilot because it avoided the receptor-copy selection required by fentanyl-bound human OPRM1 structure 8EF5. Both are 3.3 Å cryo-EM entries and structurally tractable candidates, but tractability is not compound-specific target evidence. The preference for 6VRH is not a map-quality or biological-target ranking.

### Limited docking checks

All docking calculations used AutoDock Vina 1.2.7 with a rigid receptor and the default `vina` scoring function. That function contains steric/attractive Gaussian, repulsion, hydrophobic, hydrogen-bonding, and rotatable-bond terms but no explicit partial-charge/Coulomb term. Gasteiger partial-charge sums were used only as preparation checks. Empirical scores were not interpreted as free energies, affinities, or probabilities.

**ACC2 pilot (exp-002).** The human ACC2 carboxyltransferase-domain structure 3TDC and its symmetry-spanning `0EU` site were inspected. Three native-ligand searches used 20 modes each, seeds 1001–1003, four configured CPUs, exhaustiveness 8, and a rigid, water-stripped PISA assembly-2 model. RMSD was measured over stereochemistry-preserving graph automorphisms directly in the unchanged site frame, with no ligand fitting. The protocol required stopping before PRL-8-53 or comparison docking if native ranking was not interpretable.

**SERT reference pilot (exp-003).** The exact model used resolved SERT chain A from 6VRH, residues 77–617. Incomplete Asn145 and Lys201 were deleted, leaving uncapped local gaps; missing termini were not built. Fab, two N-acetylglucosamines, detergent, and chloride were removed, and no sodium or water was added. All histidines used template HIE assignments without a pKa calculation. The fixed box was defined by deposited paroxetine plus 6 Å per face. One independently generated conformer for each neutral and protonated paroxetine preparation was searched with three fixed seeds, 20 modes per search, two configured CPUs, and exhaustiveness 8. Charge and starting conformer differed together, preventing a causal charge comparison.

**Exploratory PRL-8-53 calculation (exp-004).** The byte-identical exp-003 receptor and box were reused. Two independently generated starting conformers for each neutral and protonated state were combined with three seeds, yielding 12 fixed searches and 240 modes. The starting pairs sampled opposite signs of a coordinate-derived tertiary-nitrogen pyramidal determinant. Those signs are modeling assumptions, not invented permanent enantiomers. Pairwise pose RMSDs were calculated within state, without coordinate fitting, over complete graph automorphisms. Cross-state pairs were not pooled. The initial graph-only seed-selection preflight did not retain its selector, candidate-seed list, or trial record; the committed geometries can be regenerated, but their original selection history cannot be reconstructed as observed evidence.

## Results

### Historical evidence did not identify a molecular target

The retained identity records establish PRL-8-53's chemical identity. The inspected 1978 abstract and patent report limited historical behavioral, tissue, and toxicity observations [1,3]. Neither those inspected materials nor the recorded bounded searches established a PRL-8-53 Ki, Kd, target-specific IC50, binding pose, pharmacokinetic or brain-exposure measurement, or compound-specific pKa. The 1974 article body was not accessed, so this statement does not characterize its contents. It is a bounded account of retained material, not proof that no measurement exists anywhere. Behavioral or tissue effects cannot distinguish direct binding from indirect pharmacology.

### Prediction produced a dependent shortlist, not a biological order

SwissTargetPrediction's leading output was ACC2/ACACB, with a conditional score of about 0.71. The neutral and protonated 100-row exports had the same SHA-256 hash and were byte-identical, despite distinct accepted structures; these two submissions constitute one model result without observed representation sensitivity rather than replication. They do not demonstrate general protonation insensitivity. Five subsequent ranks were OPRM1, KCNJ1, BCHE, KCNH2, and SLC6A4/SERT, with reported conditional scores of 0.4058, 0.3039, 0.3010, 0.3004, and 0.2695. These values do not form a calibrated biological ordering.

|---|---|---|---|
| ACC2 / ACACB | Enzyme (acetyl-CoA carboxylase) | Rank 1 conditional prediction; nearest source compounds have ACC2 enzyme-inhibition records; CT-domain structures exist | No PRL assay; dependent model/source corpus; source inhibition is not binding; the 2D driver's site is unknown; native-reference pilot failed and produced no PRL score |
| OPRM1 | Family A GPCR | Rank 2 prediction; nearest 2D source ligand preserves a meta methyl benzoate and has a curated human-receptor Ki; human fentanyl-bound 8EF5 is tractable | Source ligand is a rigid piperidine from the same series as the nearest 3D ligand; no PRL assay; 8EF5 has two receptor/ligand copies and no PRL calculation |
| KCNJ1 / ROMK | Inward-rectifier K+ channel | Rank 3 prediction; source ligands have cell-based flux/electrophysiology inhibition | Assays are not PRL data or direct equilibrium binding; chemotypes differ; no PRL follow-up |
| BCHE | Hydrolase (butyrylcholinesterase) | Rank 4 prediction; source benzyl ethers have enzyme-inhibition records | Source ligands are secondary amines without PRL's ester; same publication drives both nearest examples; no PRL follow-up |
| KCNH2 / hERG | Voltage-gated K+ channel | Rank 5 prediction; source ligands have binding/electrophysiology counterscreen records | One source ligand is shared with KCNJ1; database density may reflect routine counterscreening; no PRL assay or follow-up |
| SLC6A4 / SERT | Neurotransmitter transporter | Rank 6 prediction; source ligands include a phenyltropane and an aromatic-ester compound with measured SERT activity; human paroxetine-bound 6VRH is tractable | Analogy and tractability are not PRL evidence; exploratory PRL poses are uncalibrated and preparation-sensitive |

For ACC2, the nearest 2D source active, CHEMBL3928386, had FP2 similarity 0.764706 and a reported 524 nM inhibition IC50. The patent describes a recombinant human ACC2 construct lacking the N-terminal 128 residues and a coupled PK/LDH/NADH assay [4]. ChEMBL separately labels the assay organism *Homo sapiens* but leaves its taxonomy and variant fields null and assigns relationship `H` and confidence 8; the reason is not recorded, and the patent's human-construct disclosure does not erase that curation caveat. No coupling-enzyme counterscreen, ACC1 result, binding site, or direct affinity was established. The nearest 3D source active, CHEMBL1910396 (compound 25c), had similarity 0.830, ACC2 IC50 750 nM, and censored ACC1 IC50 >10,000 nM [5]. The retained activity rows map the targets and organisms to human ACC2 and ACC1, respectively; the retained ACC2 assay has a direct confidence-9 assignment. The censored ACC1 value supports only an apparent >13-fold ratio, not an exact selectivity value. Compound 25c itself was not co-crystallized; a related compound 31 is `0EU` in 3TDC.

The other target suggestions were similarly analog-driven. OPRM1's nearest 2D ligand had a curated Ki of 850 nM [6]; ChEMBL maps the activity to a human target and describes cloned human receptor, but the assay organism/taxonomy fields are null and the target assignment is `H`/8. KCNJ1's nearest 2D ligand had an 89 nM human-cell rubidium-efflux IC50 with direct assignment (`D`/9) and a 26 nM patch-clamp IC50 whose assay organism/taxonomy fields are null and assignment is `H`/8 [7]. The same compound had a 1,900 nM KCNH2 radioligand-displacement record with direct human assignment (`D`/9). BCHE's nearest audited ligands had 2,000 and 500 nM enzyme-inhibition IC50 values from one publication, both direct human assignments (`D`/9) [8]. SERT source records included a 210 nM uptake IC50 with direct human assignment [9], a 0.70 nM radioligand-displacement Ki for a rigid phenyltropane whose assay organism/taxonomy fields are null and assignment is `H`/8 [10], and 3,650 nM binding Ki plus 2,350 nM uptake IC50 for an aromatic methyl-ester analog, both direct human assignments [11]. These values describe other compounds in different assays. They cannot be transferred to PRL-8-53.

### The ACC2 setup failed its native-reference check

The 3TDC site crossed a crystallographic symmetry interface: 14 of 20 protein residues within 4.5 Å of deposited `0EU` came from the symmetry mate. In three fixed searches, the top-scored poses were 9.0039–9.0090 Å from the deposited pose. Those wrong top-ranked poses were themselves nearly identical across seeds; repeated sampling therefore reproduced the wrong orientation rather than the deposited geometry. The closest saved pose was 3.0195 Å at rank 10 for seed 1001; closest poses for seeds 1002 and 1003 were 5.4139 and 5.2461 Å, so the closer family was not recovered consistently. No numerical pass threshold was declared. The observed native-geometry mismatch and ranking—not inconsistency of the top wrong pose—made the preparation uninterpretable as a native-ranking reference. The experiment therefore stopped before PRL-8-53 docking. This failure applies to that pocket preparation, not every ACC2 pocket, and it neither tests nor excludes PRL-8-53 binding. The closest 2D analog's site remains unknown.

### Paroxetine recovery supported only one SERT model's internal geometry

The 6VRH coordinate audit found one complete, full-occupancy paroxetine copy and a locally complete 15-residue contact inventory, while also recording missing termini, absent side-chain atoms elsewhere, no modeled waters or sodium, and uninspected maps. Under the frozen stripped receptor, each of six searches returned 20 modes. For neutral paroxetine, all three closest modes were rank 1 with site-frame RMSDs 0.5572, 0.5579, and 0.6189 Å. For protonated paroxetine, the corresponding rank-1 RMSDs were 1.6352, 1.6207, and 1.6535 Å. Thus all six closest modes were top ranked. The one-conformer-per-state design and different seeds confound charge, geometry, and sampling; the result does not establish a preferred paroxetine protonation state or calibrate screening performance.

### Exploratory PRL-8-53 poses were preparation-sensitive

The fixed exp-004 matrix completed 12 searches and retained all 240 modes. Neutral scores ranged from −8.729 to −5.734, and protonated scores from −7.601 to −5.860 in Vina's displayed units. Scores were not interpreted as energies or affinities. The 14,280 within-state pair calculations included all ranks, not only favorable modes.

Neutral c1 top poses differed by 0.2058–0.8807 Å across seeds. Neutral c2 produced two nearly identical top poses (0.0849 Å apart) but a third 6.0802–6.0825 Å away, with a different top score; across neutral starts, the median top-pose distance was 6.1833 Å. Protonated top poses varied less in this sample: within-start differences were 0.0927–1.1159 Å, and the nine across-start top-pose distances were 1.1642–1.4109 Å (median 1.3107 Å). This is an internally more consistent sampled output, not evidence favoring physiological protonation, SERT binding, or a biological pose.

Every saved pose retained its assigned coordinate-derived nitrogen sign. Opposite signs encode starting-coordinate assumptions; geometric correspondence across them is not proof of chirality-preserving equivalence or stable stereoisomer identity. No experimental PRL-8-53 pose exists in the retained evidence against which these outputs could be judged.

## Discussion and limitations

The main result is a separation of hypotheses by why they remain unresolved. ACC2 is the leading conditional prediction, but that position rests on model similarity to source inhibitors rather than PRL-8-53 measurements. The best 2D source result is a coupled-enzyme IC50 with distinct construct, taxonomy/assignment, interference, and unknown-site caveats. The CT-pocket pilot could not support a PRL comparison. SERT has a tractable human known-ligand structure and a successful paroxetine reference calculation in one exact model, but those facts do not raise SERT to an experimentally supported PRL target. Exploratory PRL poses merely show that selected prepared molecules can be placed repeatably—or inconsistently—inside a paroxetine-centered rigid box. OPRM1, KCNJ1, BCHE, and KCNH2 remain analog-driven predictions without PRL-specific follow-up. Lack of a follow-up calculation is not a negative binding result.

No calibrated cross-target ordering can be manufactured from these heterogeneous observations. Prediction scores, source-compound IC50 or Ki values, native-reference geometry, and PRL docking scores are not commensurate. Shared source compounds and publications reduce apparent independence. Vina scores cannot establish affinity, potency, efficacy, selectivity, safety, or binding probability, and cross-molecule comparison with paroxetine is not a potency comparison. No biological RMSD, contact, or score threshold was calibrated or applied.

The structural models impose consequential limitations. The SERT receptor was rigid and stripped, with missing termini, uncapped deletions of incomplete Asn145 and Lys201, no sodium or waters, removed chloride, template residue protonation, and a known-site box. Maps were not inspected. The ACC2 model removed waters and fixed protonation and assembly choices. A modeled coordinate is not the complete biological system, and a missing coordinate does not prove lack of influence. Native-pose recovery checks setup geometry; it cannot validate unknown ligands.

Chemical-state uncertainty is also unresolved. A tertiary aliphatic amine motivates neutral and protonated sensitivity models, but no compound-specific PRL-8-53 pKa or physiological populations were identified. Vina's default score lacks an explicit Coulomb term, so partial-charge sums are bookkeeping rather than electrostatic evidence. State preparations also differed in starting geometry, hydrogens, and atom typing. The protonated sample's lower variability cannot be assigned causally to charge.

The literature review was bounded. Full texts of the historical human and preclinical papers were not accessed, and an abstract is not a full-paper review. No inference about the unread 1974 article body was made from its citation metadata. Database zero results and bounded web-index checks do not prove global absence. Generated notes carrying legacy `primary-excerpt` filenames and a withdrawn alignment were not primary evidence and were removed. Repository hashes establish byte identity, not biological truth.

The computational record improves auditability but does not amount to turnkey or externally preregistered reproduction. Protocol and input commits establish repository order and locally frozen choices, not independent timestamps. The initial exp-004 conformer seed-selection trials were not retained. The Vina binary, virtual environment, and complete dependency lock are not vendored; their temporary worktree path may disappear. Thread counts were configured, while memory records were sampled per process or child tree and did not enforce or measure a hard project-wide aggregate limit. Exp-004's nested `nice -n 19` calls yielded effective nice 20, a lower-priority deviation; no rerun was performed.

A future empirical program could test the shortlisted hypotheses using direct, compound-authenticated binding or functional assays with suitable interference controls and measured chemical-state conditions. Such tests should begin with experimentally defined assay systems rather than treating the present ranking as calibrated. These are proposals, not performed results or clinical recommendations.

## Conclusions

The bounded evidence does not establish a molecular binding target for PRL-8-53. ACC2, SERT, OPRM1, KCNJ1, BCHE, and KCNH2 remain hypotheses for different and non-comparable reasons. The ACC2 setup was stopped before PRL-8-53 docking; the SERT setup recovered paroxetine but yielded PRL-8-53 outputs that were limited, preparation-sensitive model poses. The useful outcome is an auditable shortlist, explicit failure evidence, and a map of unresolved questions—not target confirmation or a calibrated biological ranking.
